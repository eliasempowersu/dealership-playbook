01

Days 1–2 · Numbers first

See the store clearly

You can't fix what you can't see. Before touching anything, get the store's real numbers on one board that everybody can read at a glance.

  • Sales log access on day one.The desk log / sales sheet is the store's truth. Everything else is opinion.
  • Build the War Room board. MTD units, total gross, new gross, used gross — refreshed several times a day.
  • Rank people units-first, then gross. Halves count on split deals.
  • Every deal, itemized: front, back, total.The bleeders hide in the averages.
  • Activity table, pulled separately: calls, texts, appointments by person.The sales log never shows effort. Effort is where coaching lives.
  • Day counter on the board — "Day __ of __ selling days" — so pace is never a guess.
  • Know the factory target AND the real objective.At Kia, 25 was October's target; 60 a month was the objective. Confusing the two poisons every conversation.

"The board doesn't manage anybody. It just makes lying to yourself impossible."

02

Where the money leaks first

Leads & CRM

Most stores don't have a traffic problem. They have a follow-up problem wearing a traffic costume. Fix the pipes before buying more water.

  • Audit the lead rotation. Who's on it, who shouldn't be — part-timers off.
  • Test-lead your own store. Submit one and time the first real response — not the auto-reply.
  • Fix the auto-responder. It must never say "sorry we missed you." It sells the car: info filled in, photo in, offer to reserve it, push texting.
  • After-hours coverage. A 7 PM or Sunday lead still gets a real response.At Kia this took a brand-new rule — half the week's leads arrived after the rule's window closed.
  • 15-minute escalation: NOTIFY ONLY. Never auto-reassign.The customer already got an email in the first salesperson's name. Switching people mid-thread breaks it.
  • Templates 1–6 saved and numbered, on a 12-hour cadence. No website links — the customer already clicked the car.
  • Saturday before-open / after-close coverage — the gap nobody sees until a lead dies in it.
  • Audit by ACTIVITY, never by status.The status dropdown is one manual field nothing automatic moves. "New" leads at Kia had calls, texts and emails all over them. Overdue-task count is the honest signal.
  • Manager follow-up email to every active new-car lead — build it and send it in the same session.
  • eBlast the overdue follow-ups with a short personal check-in.221 people were waiting at Kia. That list is found money.
  • Run the Resurrection pipeline: every lead on a stage board — New → Working → Engaging → Negotiation → Appointment → Sold. "Deals Working" = Engaging or above.

⭐ Check every phone number — store & people

Every number that rings the store — website, Google, ads, signatures, listings — verified: right number, right department, no strays (one audit found five different numbers in the wild). Every salesperson's number tracking in Purple Cloud — untracked calls and texts are invisible, and invisible activity gets coached as laziness. Every number correct in the CRM — it feeds the phone merge field in every lead email that goes out. Then call the numbers yourself and hear where they land.

Ask for workflow-editor access on day one.At Kia the 12-hour cadence died because the account had no editor and it took a support case just to ask. It was the single biggest blocker of the two weeks. Don't let it be yours.

03

Gross is made — or given away — here

The Desk

No new titles. No reorganization. Just standing rules, enforced by whoever is sitting in the tower.

  • Standing desk rule, enforced by whoever's in the tower. Every deal gets worked — fully.
  • No negative-front used deal leaves without a manager's eyes on it first.
  • Real trade ACV + manager review BEFORE numbers are presented.Kia's biggest front losses weren't bad cars. They were over-allowances that penciled a new-car deal.
  • Protect the finance presentation.While front is thin, the back end carries the store.
  • Phone standard: take control → do discovery → answer the buying signal → close the appointment. No order-taking.

"Real ACV. Not the number that pencils the deal."

05

Inventory & the lot

Feed the front line

The lot is the store's biggest billboard, and most stores park it by accident. Inventory health on the numbers side — theater on the street side.

  • Pull inventory health: units in stock, average days, age buckets.Reconcile when the system's own reports disagree — at Kia they said 17, 20 and 21 used on the same day.
  • Price the aged units first.Kia had 9 over 60 days; the oldest sat 207 days at 93% of market, waiting on a decision, not a discount.
  • Whole-lot pricing vs market — how many above, how many below, where the lot sits as a whole.
  • Check new-car pricing against the front target before the month starts.Only 1 of 102 priced units at Kia carried $2,000 over cost. A $1,500 front average was uphill before the first up.
  • Owner-retention dials: 20 calls + 40 texts a day to owners near payoff — with an urgency, hook, value and CTA script, not just dials.

⭐ Lot arrangement — a process with an owner

The front line is an advertisement: the most colorful cars up front where the road sees them. Signs and balloons fresh — faded signage reads as a closed store. Hoods open on the front row — it says "open for business" from the street. Upbeat music outside, chill music inside — energy on the lot, calm at the desk. And the rule that makes it a process instead of a cleanup: when a front-line car sells or delivers, the next colorful unit rotates in the same day. The arrangement never decays back to whatever got parked there.

"Does the store look open and alive from the road?"At Kia the answer was no — dead neon sign, a downslope hiding the inventory. The owner wouldn't fix the sign with a new store coming, so the visibility play was manufacturer-paid Christmas lights. Work the problem you actually have.

06

⭐ Elias's addition — day one

The website: visible & built to sell

The website is the store's real front door — more customers walk through it in a day than through the glass. Run the full visibility + sales-capture report on day one.

  • Visibility: indexed and fresh? Ranking for dealer and service searches — or do the aggregators own your used cars?
  • One clean Google identity.Reviews split across names, five phone numbers in the wild, a description naming the wrong city — all found in one audit, all fixable in a week.
  • Route every review ask to the Google profile.
  • Chat/text widget on the homepage, inventory AND vehicle pages.Its absence was the #1 sales leak in the Mercedes audit.
  • "Schedule Test Drive" on every card and every vehicle page.
  • Transparent pricing, shown not gated — MSRP, fee, final price.
  • Short ePrice form — name + email, phone optional.
  • Value Your Trade with an INSTANT valuation. Never a form that demands contact info before it gives anything.
  • Payment calculator ON the vehicle page — not buried in the nav.
  • Soft-pull pre-approval.
  • Service specials page with real, itemized specials — it drives fixed-ops traffic, not slogans.
  • A named human + direct text line on inventory — "Text ___ @ ___".
  • Verify on an actual phone: tap-to-call, tap-to-text, the sticky bar. Desktop-perfect means nothing.

"Make sure it's visible — and make sure it leads to sales."

  • Working model: $1,500 front + $1,000 back at 40 cars.
  • Get the pack off a deal recap. Don't guess it.And don't build math on the $799 — that's a processing fee added on the statement, not the pack.
  • Sales payroll vs gross — Kia's model ran $62K/month of sales payroll against the target.
  • Get the monthly statement: service + parts gross and fixed-ops pay complete the picture.At Kia: 5 techs, 3 advisors, service and parts managers, 2 clerks, 2 detailers — and the numbers never arrived. The model stayed half-built.
  • Parse the factory incentive pack on day one — rates, customer cash, lease cash, stair-step money — so "what's on this car?" takes seconds, not a phone call.
08

Basics & the handoff

Leave it running

The test of a turnaround isn't what happens while you're there. It's what survives you leaving.

  • Wiring instructions sheet, dealership-branded.A customer wiring money needs it the day they ask — not after a scramble through another store's letterhead.
  • Paperwork basics confirmed with the office before the first deal needs them.
  • Write the handoff for the person staying: what changed, what's still blocked, who owns what.
  • The board keeps updating without you.
  • Blockers written down with case numbers — not left in your head.

The honest page

Wanted at Kia — blocked

Six things the last store never got. They're in this playbook so the next store does.

  1. The 12-hour follow-up cadence — no workflow editor in the account. Ask for access on day one.
  2. Saturday early/late lead coverage — spec written, never built.
  3. The manager email to ~88 active leads — built, saved, then eaten by the blast tool on reopen. Build and send in one session.
  4. The eBlast to 221 overdue follow-ups — drafted, never sent.
  5. The monthly statement — service and parts numbers never arrived.
  6. The pack amount — still unknown. Get it off a deal recap in week one.
